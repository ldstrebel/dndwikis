Chapter 1: The Night Of Embers

Rain fell through the colossal leaves in heavy, rhythmic sheets, filling the petrified canopy with a sound like distant drumming. Fifty feet below, the forest floor was lost in twilight shadow.

High in the woven limbs of four ancient petrified giants, the Canopy Raft strained against its high-tension bungees. Ignatious leaned over the wooden gunwale, blowing a plume of grey ash through his nostrils. Faint orange embers danced along the cuffs of his heavy leather coat, fighting off the damp chill.

"Well,"

Ignatious grunted, adjusting the warhammer strapped across his back.

"The landing went bettah than I thought."

Beside him, Loami unbolted the central winch casing. He wiped grease and rainwater from his brow with the back of a calloused hand, adjusting his damp woolen flat cap.

"Gotta say I'm shocked we made it up here at all,"

Loami said, his broad shoulders tensing as he checked the rig's balance.

"We look a little like a ragtag crew, but here we are. Safe as we're gonna get."

A low, resonant thud vibrated through the petrified wood beneath their boots. Miles away, through gaps in the foliage, a jagged pillar of violet lightning hammered the central Mon. The air tasted sharply of copper and ozone.

Ignatious squinted toward the flash.

"What's the purpose of all this? Is the Academy testing our adventurism, or is there a reason the sky keeps tryin' to melt the obelisk?"

Loami's expression darkened. Before entering the Academy, he had spent years servicing power conduits in Harmony, learning the temperamental pulse of Aether technology.

"They've lost control,"

Loami murmured, inspecting the hum of their craft's instruments.

"The nodes broadcast a high-frequency pulse to keep canopy beasts at bay. But whatever hit the bay short-circuited the main line. The shields are down. Recovery time... eight increments, minimum."

He looked back at the party.

"We've got three clocks tickin' against us now: how long we can survive out here, when the storm surge hits, and whether this raft holds together."

Ignatious ran a gloved hand along a thick, fibrous vine clinging to the petrified trunk.

"Can I grab some of these vines for extra ropage? You can always use more rope."

"Hop off if you want,"

Loami said, scanning the dark canopy.

"I'm gonna scout."

Ignatious swung his boot over the rail, stepping onto a wide, moss-slick bough. But as his weight shifted, the dark foliage rustled unnaturally. He froze, clutching the vine.

"No, no,"

Ignatious muttered, his eyes darting through the leaves.

"There's always somethin' hidin' out there waitin' to snap at you when you get close. All right, guys... I'm grabbin' some vines, but keep your eyes open. You nevah know when you'll need more rope."

Loami crouched low on the bough, narrowing his dark eyes. His low-light vision pierced the rain-dappled twilight. Beneath the creeping vines, sleek, four-legged silhouettes prowled across the petrified bark—lean, cat-like predators perfectly camouflaged against the dark timber.

"Hey!"

Loami hissed, dropping his center of gravity.

"We've got feline creatures out on the branches! Natty, hurry back!"

Britt and Aggie scrambled up beside him, peering into the shadows. The creatures were built somewhere between a coyote and a mountain cat—territorial predators native to the upper canopy, known as Meowders.

"A coyobi? Interesting,"

Ignatious mused.

Britt drew her hunting knife, her green skin shimmering faintly in the dim light.

"Oh yeah, we know those! They're in our forest back home—Meowders!"

"Seen what?"

Ignatious asked, blinking.

"What did you see?"

A sudden flurry of scurrying claws echoed across the bark. Dozens of glowing orange eyes ignited in the dark leaves surrounding the party.

At the rear of the raft, Iggy sat curled in a tight, shivering ball inside his heavy wool trench coat. His round copper goggles were slick with rain, his small clay-and-soil body rocking back and forth in sheer terror.

"The treeth have eyeth!"

Iggy whimpered, clutching his head.

"The treeth have eyeth!"

"Those aren't trees, Iggy!"

Aggie called back.

"They're basically just mean cats!"

Britt didn't hesitate.

"Natty—move over, I've got a plan!"

she shouted, vaulting past Ignatious.

She raised her hands and unleashed *Hypnotic Shimmer*. A brilliant cascade of pulsing chromatic light erupted across the bough. The radiant illusion dazzled the pack of Meowders, leaving them pawing blindly at the glowing air.

"Are they dazed?!"

Ignatious called out.

"They're stunned!"

Britt shouted back.

"Move while they're dazed!"

"Do we try to move the raft or lower a branch?"

Loami asked, eyeing their position.

"With lightning and thunder like this?"

Ignatious countered.

"Can we get away, or are these things gonna pursue us with bloodlust?"

"They're territorial,"

Aggie warned.

"They won't back off easily."

"I'm gonna try somethin' else,"

Ignatious said.

"Just brandishin' the flames a bit! Let's see how these cats like my dreadful visage!"

Fanning the inner furnace of his chest, he activated *Dread Visage*. A terrifying, roaring face of orange fire erupted over his features.

Loami grinned, stepping beside him with his spanner raised.

"All right, you little cats! Bring it on!"

The explosion of fire shattered the nerve of several Meowders. Screeching in terror, two of the creatures scrambled back into the foliage. But the alpha snarled, lunging forward with reckless fury, claws raking across Loami's shoulder pauldron.

"Psh, these little cats can only do minor damage!"

Loami grunted, bracing as his armor absorbed the strike.

Chapter 2: The Cazito Maneuver
