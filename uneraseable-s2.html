THE MARGIN, THE FRAGMENTS & THE LOST ROADS

Inside the timeless refuge of The Margin, Theodore reveals the sanctuary's 1846 origins while Naomi organizes a corrector drill and sets a course across the Lost Roads for a lost Greek artifact.

Part 1

CHAPTER 13: THE SANCTUARY OF FRESH INK

● Critique Added

Outside the entrance to the secluded Appalachian retreat, Pierre lingered near the timber archway, peering cautiously through his round spectacles while Dravin and Eusacles stepped forward.

● Critique Added

A middle-aged man in casual flannel waved with an easy, welcoming grin. "Don't be scared, bros," he called out warmly. "Don't be scared at all. We love fresh ink around here. We're happy to have you."

Prof. Edward Dravin ● Critique Added

"Would we have remembered dying?" Dravin asked, adjusting his spectacles.

Mike ● Critique Added

"You're not actually dead, but it depends on who you ask," the man chuckled, introducing himself as Mike. Hiding behind his leg was a shy young girl with solemn eyes. "I made it to the Margin around 2009. I was forty-two, and I've stayed forty-two. This is Lily—she's been eleven for a long time."

● Critique Added

Eusacles closed his eyes, invoking his divine senses. A natural twenty flared in his blood, revealing the Margin not as a simple campground, but as a consecrated refuge shrouded by the mythological Mist, where residents carried faint, residual auras of both celestial and undead power.

Part 2

CHAPTER 14: ECHOES OF THE SEA

● Critique Added

Mike led the group past a row of rustic timber cabins, discussing the lodgings and community.

Pierre ● Critique Added

As they walked, Alfie suddenly tensed in Pierre's rucksack, his tiny wooden hands gripping the canvas rim. His painted eyes glazed over with haunting recollection: "I remember the dark... and the quiet... and lots of water, and many men screaming and shouting..."

Pierre ● Critique Added

Pierre gently rested a finger on Alfie's shoulder, murmuring softly: "Alfie... Alfie, it's okay. Bring it back."

Alfie ● Critique Added

"...and everyone going away under the water," Alfie finished quietly with a shudder.

Part 3

CHAPTER 15: THE PATERNAL TETHER

Pierre ● Critique Added

"Bring it back, mate," Pierre whispered softly to Alfie. "You are not on the ship anymore."

Eusacles ● Critique Added

Mike smiled with quiet compassion. "This is a fun doll," he observed, before turning his gaze toward Eusacles's wrist. "Who gave you that watch? I'm getting some sort of sense from it."

Eusacles ● Critique Added

Eusacles glanced down at the heavy black timepiece. "As far as I know, it was a gift from my dad, given to me by my mother when I went off on my own. I never knew him."

Mike ● Critique Added

"Fascinating," Mike nodded.

Part 4

CHAPTER 16: THE NIGHTTIME INTRUSION

● Critique Added

Settling into their guest cabin, the party prepared for sleep.

● Critique Added

Suddenly, the wooden door swung open. A young woman with the side of her head shaved and eyes blazing with manic academic energy burst inside. "Sorry I'm late!" she announced breathlessly. "I heard there were new people! I am knee-deep in research and you all could really help out! Come on, I'll show you what I'm doing."

Part 5

CHAPTER 17: SATURDAY PANCAKES AND CONSPIRACY BOARDS

Pierre ● Critique Added

Pierre, already sitting in his nightwear with cucumber slices prepared for his face care routine, raised an eyebrow. "It can wait until the morning. I have already started my face care. I only have so many cucumbers."

● Critique Added

Pierre gently closed the cabin door on the frantic researcher until the next day.

Naomi ● Critique Added

The next morning, a knock sounded at the door. Mike grinned from the porch. "Got some pancakes going—my thing on Saturdays! Naomi has been in my ear about getting you guys to check out what she's got going on."

Mike ● Critique Added

At breakfast, Mike shared his personal theory: "I have a card from my daughter that says 'To Dad'. That's why I think I'm here. Something concrete was written down."

Theodore (Teddy) ● Critique Added

After breakfast, they entered Naomi's cabin. "Early on, Teddy was big into the fragments," she explained, leading them to her workspace. Her cot was neglected in favor of old CRT computer monitors streaming green code and a massive wall covered in pinned photos and red yarn connecting mythic events.

Part 6

CHAPTER 18: THE MYTHIC FRAGMENTS

Theodore (Teddy) ● Critique Added

Inside the cabin, Naomi explained her algorithm for tracking temporal anomalies. "The fragments are leftovers from these crazy edits," she told them, gesturing to her notes. "Theodore told me they have some sort of energy attached to them—when he touched one, he experienced visions of different realities."

Naomi ● Critique Added

She pulled up an online academic thread showing a picture of the newly discovered Greek tablet on display in a museum. "There is a ton of discourse about what it translates to say," Naomi pointed out.

Prof. Edward Dravin ● Critique Added

Dravin adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses in astonishment. "I actually wrote a paper on this tablet!"

● Critique Added

Naomi scrolled down the screen, showing the fierce online debate and op-eds surrounding Dravin's research.

Part 7

CHAPTER 19: THE STOLEN TOMES & THE LOST ROADS

Prof. Edward Dravin ● Critique Added

"Where is the closest fragment?" Dravin asked, studying the map pins.

● Critique Added

Naomi pointed out three active leads: a mythic toy catapult in a UK pawn shop, a doctor giving a book tour, and the ancient Greek limestone tablet at the state museum in North Carolina.

● Critique Added

Pierre pulled out the ancient books he had taken from the Library of the Fates.

Pierre ● Critique Added

"Books are for the public to read," Pierre insisted innocently with a Gallic shrug.

Pierre ● Critique Added

Examining an ancient Greek passage in the volume, Naomi and Dravin translated a reference to the **Lost Roads**—ancient shifting planar pathways tied to mythic origins. "This book mentions the Lost Roads... and in a way, we are on a lost road now," Pierre noted thoughtfully.

Part 8

CHAPTER 20: THE RALEIGH TARGET

Pierre ● Critique Added

Pierre examined the photos of the limestone artifact. "This tablet is something I can see as a reason I am here."

Eusacles ● Critique Added

"Let's start with the Greek tablet," Eusacles agreed. "At least we have the potential of the good old doctor translating it."

Prof. Edward Dravin ● Critique Added

"This tablet," Dravin nodded eagerly, his academic curiosity fully ignited.

Part 9

CHAPTER 21: THE DRY RUN

Alfie ● Critique Added

Before embarking on their mission, the group tested their coordination during a brief supply run. Alfie drew his needle rapier, indignantly protesting anytime anyone referred to him as a doll: "Quit calling me a doll!"

● Critique Added

Another truck pulled up carrying Mike, Teddy, Rosa, and Anna, all laughing heartily at the spirited exchange. Returning to the Margin's porch, Naomi was there to welcome them back, confirming everyone was ready.

Part 10

CHAPTER 22: AT THE THRESHOLD OF THE LOST ROADS

● Critique Added

The party gathered at the edge of the Margin near the maintenance shed, standing on the edge of the threshold to begin their journey through the Lost Roads toward North Carolina.

● Critique Added

Pierre walked over to Dravin and Eusacles, adjusting his rucksack as they prepared to step into the shifting dimensional mist.

✦ End of Session 2 ✦

#2 Atmosphere & World-Building vs. Dialogue Volume Story Choice

Alternative Style:

Dialogue-Heavy Exchanges — Having characters talk through all exposition and reactions.

#3 Scene Length & Reading Rhythm Story Choice
Choice Made:

Episodic Scene Bites — Fast-paced, modular scenes optimized for mobile reading and smooth audio flow.

Long-Form Sprawling Chapters — Extended 20-page chapters bundling multiple encounters together.

The Trade-off: Gives readers clear milestones and natural stopping points rather than unbroken long blocks.
#4 Live Table Canon vs. Fiction Smoothing Story Choice
Choice Made:

Faithful to Player Actions & Rolls — Every spontaneous roll, wild idea, and table decision is canon.

Alternative Style:

The Trade-off: Honors true tabletop agency and dice spontaneity while framing it in rich prose.
